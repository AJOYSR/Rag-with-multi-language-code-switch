## Objective
"We separate answering from abstention in a context-based question answering task, where a language model answers a question about a document in a possibly different language or abstains when the document contains no answer" (Abstract).

## Data (§3, Table 1)
- **TyDiQA:** 11 passage languages, Bengali among them. The authors also built a cross-lingual extension with questions machine-translated into 18–23 languages (Hy-MT2-7B, MADLAD-400).
- **BELEBELE/unanswerable:** negatives built "by removing the sentences that contain the gold label span" (§3). This is the same minimal-deletion idea as our insufficient-evidence items.
- **UDHR:** questions generated by an LLM and checked "by an independent pass"; 18 of 90 items are unanswerable.
- **Test/silver:** labels produced by an LLM, used for evaluation only.
- **Scripts:** every input is in its language's native script. The word "romanized" appears only in the output-scoring rubric (GlotLID). **There are no romanized or code-mixed inputs.**

## Method (§4)
- A linear probe on frozen layer 19 of Qwen3.5-9B decides answerability. A LoRA adapter, trained only on answerable questions, writes the answers.
- The abstention decision is **not** an instructed or verbalized LLM abstention. It is read from internal activations.
- Thresholds: the authors compare a single global threshold with per-language log-odds offsets, which are "equivalent to a per-language threshold" (§5.2). They evaluate with macro-F1, false abstentions and the spread of the offsets.

## Key findings (with locations)
1. Ranking transfers but the threshold does not. The probe's "ranking of answerable against unanswerable questions transfers across languages, corpora, and negative constructions it was not trained on, but its decision threshold does not" (Abstract; also §6).
2. "A probe's direction therefore transfers to unseen data and languages more easily than its threshold does" (§5.2).
3. For the TyDi-only probe on test/silver, the per-language offsets have a spread of 0.959, against a noise floor of 0.215, "a per-language bias of up to 3.5 log-odds" (§5.2, Table 5, Fig. 1).
4. Training on in-domain multilingual data, not recalibrating, closes most of the gap. The spread falls to 0.451. With a single global threshold, macro-F1 rises from .697 to .896, while per-language offsets give only "+0.003" and "makes 10 of 24 languages worse" (§5.2, Table 6).
5. **Bengali:** "The exception is Bengali, where the probe ties the baseline, .830 against .831" (§5.2). Bengali has the second-highest lexical-overlap baseline of the 26 languages.
6. Sharing a script with the passage adds little to the ranking: "only adds .0047 AUROC" on TyDi/cross18 (§5.2). This is the only analysis of script in the paper, and it concerns question–passage script match, not romanization.
7. Changing the question language costs −.048 AUROC when negatives are built by sentence deletion, against −.014 for natural negatives (§5.2).

## Limitations (theirs and ours)
- Most cross-lingual evaluations vary only the question language (Limitations).
- Labels are silver, generated by an LLM. This is a shared-task system paper, so the analyses are mostly single runs without significance tests.
- The thresholds are judged by F1, false abstentions and offset spread. There is no **target risk** and no risk–coverage control.
- There is one model, and the abstention signal is a probe rather than the deployed model's own behaviour.

## How our work differs (exact statement)
Krause et al. show, using an internal probe, that answerability thresholds for context-based QA do not transfer across *native-script question languages*. Our study differs in four ways:
- it varies the **script and code-mixing within one language** (English, Bengali native, romanized Bengali, code-mixed) on the same items;
- it tests the abstention mechanisms deployed systems actually use: instructed abstention, verbalized or token confidence, and a sufficiency autorater with selective generation;
- it judges transfer as **realised risk against a target risk**, per variant, with confidence intervals;
- it separates comprehension from calibration with a per-item comprehension check.

## What we must concede or cite
- **[FACT]** Answerability thresholds for context-based QA have been shown not to transfer across languages (Krause et al. 2026), so we cannot claim that cross-lingual threshold non-transfer in context-based QA is new.
- Their remedy (more in-domain multilingual training) is a probe-training intervention. Ours (per-variant thresholds, transliteration, pivot-language judgment) involves no training. Their finding that per-language offsets barely help (+0.003 macro-F1) is a **useful contrast** for our RQ4(i), so we should report whether per-variant thresholds help in our setting.
- Their probe signal is a candidate extra method, M2c (hidden-state answerability probe). [REC] Do not add it to the core design. It would need training data and hidden-state access, which is costly with Ollama. Mention it as future work.
